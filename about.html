About

I design interfaces for the moment the user knows more than the system — and I build the small, one-tap ways for them to say so.

I studied Economics at New York University, where I developed an interest in the gap between how a system is supposed to behave and how people actually use it. Economics taught me to look for the incentive underneath a decision — design gave me a way to shape it.

My work sits between product design and product thinking. I care most about the decisions that don't show up on the surface: what the default should be, what happens when the system is wrong, and what the interface does when there's nothing left to say.

Outside of design I'm reading, running, and trying to keep a stable relationship with matcha.

What I Do

Product Design

Interfaces for AI-first products, focused on the moments between the user and the model — how corrections happen, how trust is built, how a system says it's not sure.

Systems Thinking

I like design work that starts before the pixels — mapping which decision a screen is actually resolving, what a "no" costs the user, and how a pattern generalizes.

Writing About It

I write to think — short essays about design decisions, why defaults matter, and how good products treat the person on the other side of them.
